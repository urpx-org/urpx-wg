## URPX goes public: an open standard for machine-readable utility rate plans

*Hosted by LF Energy Standards and Specifications (LFESS). Led by Flux Tailor with formal NLR collaboration, URPX is developed through open collaboration under LFESS governance.*

Every device that charges, heats, stores, or sells electricity makes better decisions when it knows what electricity actually costs. Yet the prices customers pay are still published the way they were decades ago: as PDF tariff books, statements, and supplements scattered across utility websites. Software that wants to answer a simple question — *what does this customer pay for a kilowatt-hour right now?* — has to re-read those documents by hand, utility by utility, month by month.

The Utility Rate Plan Exchange (URPX) standard exists to close that gap. Today the URPX Working Group is making the standard public: an open vocabulary for describing utility rate plans in full, machine-readable detail — released at version [LAUNCH-VERSION] at [urpx.org](https://urpx.org) and on [GitHub](https://github.com/urpx-org/urpx).

### What URPX describes

URPX is an OWL ontology with SHACL validation shapes and JSON-LD as its exchange format. It models the things utilities actually publish:

- **Rate plans as durable identities.** A rate plan like Consolidated Edison's SC 1 Rate I persists for decades while its prices change. URPX separates the identity from the changing content, so references stay stable.
- **Publications as sources of truth.** Tariff books, monthly statements, supplements, and market price feeds each carry part of a rate plan's pricing. URPX models each publication and the pricing content it carries — with citations back to the source document, down to individual prices.
- **Resolved snapshots with honest coverage.** A URPX rate plan version is a self-contained snapshot of everything that applies in a time window, assembled from those publications. Every snapshot carries an explicit coverage claim — what it includes, what it resolved, and with what confidence — so consumers can tell a complete answer from a partial one.
- **The full structure of real pricing:** time-of-use schedules, seasons, tiers and brackets, eligibility and applicability conditions, demand and energy charges, holiday calendars, and customer and service-point profiles.


### Why this matters now

Electrification is making retail electricity pricing consequential for software. Electric vehicles, heat pumps, batteries, and rooftop solar all shift load — and their value to owners and to the grid depends on when and how usage is priced. Demand flexibility programs, home energy management, bill transparency tools, and utility tariff analysis all start from the same missing primitive: a trustworthy, machine-readable description of the rate plan. URPX is that primitive, offered as an open standard rather than a proprietary dataset.
